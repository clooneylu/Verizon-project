Operating rule. Each month, re-estimate the shift (and a slope) on the latest 6–8 weeks of funded plans with known outcomes. A jump in the fitted shift of more than about 0.3 means conditions have changed and the approval cut-off should be reviewed. In our test the fitted shift was +0.50 and it held for the following six months.

Also logged in a production version: every score with its inputs and model version, cut-off in force, rep override and reason, and realized outcome once known.

1. Learn from history

300,000 applications from 2023–2024. 265,060 were funded, so we know whether each customer repaid or defaulted.

2. Fit a transparent model

A logistic regression learns how credit score, income, age, customer status and plan terms move the odds of default. No race, ethnicity or gender.

3. Score each applicant

A rep types a few facts and gets a default probability and the plan’s expected profit in seconds.

4. Decide with a clear rule

Approve at 20% or below. The plan only breaks even at 41.7%, so the buffer is wide. Review and recalibrate monthly.

Where the line sits

What we found. Funded plans default at 10.7% today. Applicants funded in the 2023 promotion inside today’s deny zone defaulted at about 15% (18–23% after adjusting for the 2024 shock). The break-even is 41.7%.
Honest limits. We only see outcomes for customers Verizon funded, so the new approvals should start as a monitored pilot. The model ranks applicants only modestly better than the current score; its main value is accurate probabilities.
